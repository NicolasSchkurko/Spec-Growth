# Conclusions and limitations of the method

After a series of tests with different models, my experience indicates that Spec Growth produces more coherent results than *vibecoding* without a defined structure and than my usual experience with *spec-driven development*. However, these results are empirical observations of its use, not a controlled experimental comparison, so they do not allow me to claim a general superiority of the method.

The repository [Foco](https://github.com/NicolasSchkurko/Foco) is a use and experimentation case in which I used Spec Growth up to version 0.5.0. During its development I identified limitations that led me to review both the method and its verification mechanisms.

Spec Growth seeks to mitigate two recurring problems in AI-assisted development: the degradation of response quality as the agent's context load increases and the silent divergence between specifications and code. The change structure, traceability and automated gates allow better control of these risks, but they do not guarantee that the implementation is correct or that the final behavior matches what was expected.

A concrete example occurred when requesting the correction of a gesture in Foco. The agent modified a function related to the behavior, but not the function that was actually executed from the `lambda` associated with the gesture. Although the change appeared to address the problem, the behavior remained incorrect. This case shows that verifying the existence of changes, tests and coherent specifications does not necessarily equate to validating the actual execution path. Detecting and correcting the problem required understanding how the involved functions were connected.

Furthermore, the method does not eliminate the cognitive debt that AI-based development can generate. Documentation, explanations and teach-back can support understanding, but they do not guarantee that the developer has acquired real knowledge of the code. As acknowledged in `references/status.md`, comprehension sign-off is verifiable as an artifact, but not as a cognitive state. The responsibility for understanding, reviewing and validating the generated code remains with the developer, especially when errors require domain-specific knowledge to be recognized.

Therefore, I consider Spec Growth a promising companion method, but still not conclusive as a general approach to AI-assisted development. Its value does not lie in eliminating errors, but in providing a structure that makes decisions, changes and part of their verification conditions more explicit. It can be particularly convenient for small, bounded changes, as long as the process cost is proportionate to the risk and the complexity of the change.

The main pending limitation is that the method largely verifies that what was specified has been implemented, but does not determine by itself whether the solution is correct, whether the tests cover the relevant paths, or whether the developer really understands the result. Evaluating its general effectiveness would require more systematic comparisons, with equivalent tasks and metrics such as development time, rework, defects detected after integration and review cost.
